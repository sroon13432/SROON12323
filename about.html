About CanMySpec

CanMySpec is an independent reference site for PC game system requirements. It holds the published minimum and recommended specifications of more than 28,000 Windows titles, around 600 App Store games, and a growing set of non-Steam PC releases, on one page per game, with no account and no client to install.

What the site is for

Almost every game page on the web answers "can my PC run this?" with a copy of the store's requirement table and an advertisement. The table is necessary but rarely enough: it never says which of the two GPU columns actually matters at 1080p, whether 8 GB of memory is a bottleneck or a formality, or whether the card you own sits a whole class below the recommended one.

So each page here answers three questions in order. First, what did the publisher publish (the minimum and recommended rows, verbatim, including the resolution and frame-rate target each row was written against). Second, what does that mean for the machine in front of you: the gap between the two tiers by our relative GPU index, whether memory or storage is the real constraint, and which class of card the recommended figure belongs to. Third, what else changes the decision: the Steam Deck grade where Steam publishes one, the DirectX floor, the release year, and the genre's own habits.

Where the data comes from

  • Steam — the store's own app-details records for minimum and recommended requirements, review summary (score and review count), release date, genres, supported platforms, and the Steam Deck compatibility grade.
  • Apple App Store — listing data for iOS titles: download size, minimum iOS version, supported device count, price, screenshots and rating count.
  • PCGamingWiki, GOG and Epic Games Store — requirement rows for PC releases that are not sold on Steam.
  • Publisher pages — a small number of widely searched online games (for example League of Legends or VALORANT) whose requirements are published on the developer's own support site rather than in a store.

Each figure on a page can therefore be traced to the store record it came from. Where a store publishes nothing for a field, the row is hidden rather than filled with a guess — a page never prints "Not specified" inside a sentence, and no rating is shown without a real score and a real review count behind it.

Who writes and checks it

CanMySpec is written and maintained by one independent editor, not by a publisher, a store or an agency. Collection is automated — the catalogue is re-read from the sources above every morning and new titles are added — while the page templates, the analysis wording, the tier model and the guides are written and revised by hand. Corrections are handled by a person: when a reader reports a wrong requirement or a missing game, the page is checked against the store record and changed, and the next build carries the fix to every page that shares the template.

Nothing on this site is paid placement. No publisher, store or hardware vendor can pay for a game to be included, ranked higher, or given a kinder verdict, and no affiliate commission changes a recommendation.

What this site does not claim

We do not run our own frame-time benchmarks. Every frame-rate figure you see is either the publisher's own published target for that requirement row, or an estimate that is labelled as one. The GPU tiers, the FPS checker and the 4K estimates are derived calculations from published requirements and a relative performance index; they are honest approximations for comparing one title with another, not measurements of any particular machine. The editorial methodology sets out exactly how each of them is produced, and what it cannot tell you.

Corrections and contact

If a requirement looks wrong, a game is missing, or a page will not load, write to contact@canmyspec.com with the page address and, where you have one, a link to the publisher's page that shows the right figure. Corrections are made on the page itself, and the fix reaches the whole catalogue with the next build. The contact page lists what to include for each kind of request.

CanMySpec is financed by display advertising and, where a page links to a shop, by affiliate commission on purchases. Both are disclosed on the privacy page and in the terms of use.
