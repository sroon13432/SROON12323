Apology & Next Steps on Yi Sang and Don Quixote's JP Translation Changes

Limbus Company · posted by the game's developer on Steam·9 November 2026·Source

Yi Sang's Japanese dialogue contained elements of classical grammar that we felt made some lines difficult to follow. As a result, in recent chapters, we had instead expressed his voice through literary phrasing that incorporated Korean words and expressions. In the Oct. 29th update, we also revised his dialogue from the early story to match that style.

For Don Quixote, we used archaic sentence endings to convey her knightly manner of speaking. However, some of those endings could have come across as a parody of a samurai's speech, which led to the recent revision.

What the notes say

  • However, some of those endings could have come across as a parody of a samurai's speech, which led to the recent revision.
  • These changes led to broader adjustments to both characters' voices, but we did not clearly explain them in the patch notes before making them.
  • We failed to anticipate how confusing an unannounced change to familiar voices would be, and we realize that we should have considered the impact of the revisions more carefully.
  • Consequently, we will revert the changes to Yi Sang's and Don Quixote's speech with this update.
  • Corrections to mistranslations that do not affect their manner of speech will be retained.
  • We apologize for the confusion and inconvenience caused by the sudden changes to these elements.

Quoted from the announcement itself, shortened where a line ran long.
