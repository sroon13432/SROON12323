British Columbia: Bridges

American Truck Simulator · posted by the game's developer on Steam·13 November 2026·Source

Two of the bridges we are showcasing today can be found in Vancouver, the largest city in British Columbia. The Granville Bridge, built in 1954, connects downtown Vancouver with the southern part of the city via Granville Street, crossing False Creek along the way. The Iron Workers Memorial Bridge, meanwhile, was named in honour of the 19 workers involved in its construction, following a tragic accident on June 19, 1958, when a section of the bridge collapsed.

British Columbia is home to many stunning bridges, some set in the most scenic locations across Canada, while others hold great historical significance. Beyond their architectural appeal, these structures play an important role in connecting communities across the province's rugged landscape, spanning rivers, valleys, and waterways.

What the notes say

  • Beyond their architectural appeal, these structures play an important role in connecting communities across the province's rugged landscape, spanning rivers, valleys, and waterways.
  • The first is the Alexandra Bridge, a steel deck arch bridge that you will come across when following the Trans-Canada Highway north from the city of Hope.
  • Further north, along Highway 20, you will find the Sheep Creek Bridge, nestled between picturesque mountains.
  • If you take Highway 5 from Hope instead, you will come across the Dry Gulch Bridge, a steel box arch bridge completed in 1985, featuring a main span of 575 feet.
  • Since we already mentioned the Trans-Canada Highway, which stretches across the country from west to east, we have two more bridges there to showcase, located relatively close to each other.
  • While there, you can also spot the Canadian Pacific Railway swing bridge spanning Sicamous Narrows, which rotates to allow boats to pass through.
