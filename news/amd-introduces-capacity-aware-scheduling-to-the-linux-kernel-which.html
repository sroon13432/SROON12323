AMD introduces Capacity Aware Scheduling to the Linux kernel, which sure is the thing I'd do if I were introducing Zen 6 LP cores next year

PC Gamer · industry report·17 November 2026·Source

Up until now, AMD's performance scaling driver for Linux (amd-pstate) treated all cores as equal, assigning tasks regardless of core strength. AMD's current heterogeneous design pairs full-fat Zen 5 and lower-caste Zen 5C cores together in Zen 5 chips nowadays, but there is far less actual difference between these two core types than in the P-cores and E-cores of modern Intel architectures.

But Zen 6's full lineup is currently suggested to be quite different. Also shared in a Linux patch earlier this year, Zen 6 is set to introduce a new Low Power core type (which is set to run at a very low power, as you might guess from the name), and that is a core type that would really benefit from Capacity Aware Scheduling.

More from the report

  • What AMD has called Performance and Efficiency cores up until now is not the same as what Intel has called Performance and Efficiency cores.
  • The new Zen 6 LP cores appear to be similar to Intel's LPE (Low Power Efficiency) cores you will find in its Panther Lake chips. And as such, the need for CAS makes more sense.
  • The introduction of CAS isn't just good for the eventual introduction of AMD Zen 6 LP cores (or whatever they will be called when they launch).
  • Up until now, the extra efficiency gains from CAS would only be a bonus, but if Zen 6 LP cores are low-power enough, it could be needed for them to operate properly,
