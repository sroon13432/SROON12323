Director's Commentary - A Note on Mods

The Isle · posted by the game's developer on Steam·4 November 2026·Source

The Isle's modding community has grown a lot, and I'm thankful to everyone who puts time into building, hosting, and maintaining mods for the game (unless you offer neon skins. Please stop doing this to me and the art team). I've seen a lot of really cool mods over the years, and the community at large has always been on the same page: we're all here to have fun. However, with the recent influx of new players and the arrival of external programs for modding, it's time we had…

Mods are getting more ambitious now, and some add entirely new systems that aren't part of the game and require additional downloads. It's cool to see, and I'm not trying to discourage it, but it means more players are being asked to install things on their own computers. It'd be irresponsible of me not to provide a word of caution.

What the notes say

  • Where possible, choose mods whose source code is publicly available. Open source doesn't guarantee safety, but it lets others review what the mod actually does.
  • Only download mods from creators and sources you trust, or at least ones enough other people trust.
  • Popularity is reassuring, but it isn't proof that a mod is safe.
  • Never, ever enter your Steam login or other account details into a mod or a mod's website.
  • If you're unsure, ask in the community before installing, or skip it. If the hivemind you've all built is good for anything, it's good for answering questions in the Discord.

Quoted from the announcement itself, shortened where a line ran long.
