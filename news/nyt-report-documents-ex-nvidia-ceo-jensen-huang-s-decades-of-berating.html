NYT report documents ex-Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's decades of berating employees: 'He said he would rip both arms off a subordinate and beat the person over the head to death with them'

PC Gamer · industry report·20 November 2026·Source

It's been a strange couple of weeks for former Nvidia CEO, Jensen Huang. The once tech leader admitted to an affair, only for multiple affair allegations to surface, and he was then ousted from the company. Now, as he reportedly mounts a comeback, The New York Times has published a report detailing an alleged decades-long philosophy of getting more work out of employees "through humiliation".

Reportedly, Huang told employees that they had become so rich that "he couldn’t motivate them simply with money", so the "only way he could grind more work out of them was through humiliation".

More from the report

  • This method has reportedly been Huang's modus operandi for some time, with more than a dozen current and former employees claiming: "He has long used humiliation as a tool,
  • That humiliation allegedly took many forms. One employee reported that Huang "said he would rip both arms off a subordinate and beat the person over the head to death with them.
  • Two years ago, in a conversation with Stripe's Patrick Collison, Jensen Huang said, "I'd rather torture you into greatness, because I believe in you",
  • Huang has apparently been called out for this behaviour. In the NYT report, Michael Winston, a former senior vice president of human resources, claims he threatened to sue Nvidia in 2005,
