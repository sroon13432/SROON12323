"Silk & Silver" Dev Diary #9 - 3D Art, Music, Modding

Crusader Kings III · posted by the game's developer on Steam·3 November 2026·Source

is an expansion about people who make their living on the road, in the market, and in the council chamber of a republic. Character art had to follow that: not only more silk on dukes, but also wool on porters, fur on doges, and the hats, hoods, and pack animals that make a merchant look like a merchant.

We’re back with yet another chonkey dev diary, this time featuring 3D art, Animation, Music/Audio and Modding. As you might notice, 2D art is missing because, thanks to our talented artist's hard work, we actually had to split this dev diary into two parts (!!).

What the notes say

  • \[At the top of that ladder is the Venetian doge: a full ceremonial outfit, a furred variant, matching cloaks, and three versions of the corno ducale.]
  • Mayors can wear a formal robe in the early era, then a houppelande with a livery collar later on.
  • Early-era commoners wear practical tunics (long gowns or shorter coats), including Skjoldehamn-style hoods and a merchant’s cloak that can sit over the shoulders or come up as a hood.
  • For the Late Era, male merchants wear wool and silk cotehardies, ganaches, and houppelandes: buttoned coats, belted gowns, and longer overgowns, usually with a pouch.
  • Women wear cotehardies, houppelandes, and a sideless surcote: a fitted buttoned gown, a full robe with hanging sleeves, and an ermine-lined overdress.
  • Parti-color and patterned silk belong at court and in the merchant house; the later-era working dress, with coif and apron.

Quoted from the announcement itself, shortened where a line ran long.
