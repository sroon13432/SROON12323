My Winter Car with 8 GB RAM and an RTX 4070: playable or stuttering?

Asked by the CanMySpec Spec Desk · 24 Nov 2026 · 1 answer · Answered from the official Steam requirements
Answer from the CanMySpec database — My Winter Car, RTX 4070

Short answer: 8 GB meets the 8 GB minimum but falls short of the 16 GB recommended for this game. My Winter Car asks for 8 GB at minimum and 16 GB at recommended settings, and with RTX 4070 the memory question is separable from the graphics one.

What limits you first

  • Memory is at the minimum, not the recommendation. With 8 GB against a 16 GB recommendation, background apps (browser tabs, Discord, recording software) are competing with the game. Close them before you judge the frame rate.
  • Storage is a size question, not a speed one. The store page lists 1 GB available space; the publisher does not require an SSD, so free space is the only thing to check.
  • The CPU assumption. Every frame rate on this page assumes a processor at or above the published minimum, Intel I5 or equivalent. If yours is older, the GPU figure is the ceiling and the CPU decides how far below it you land.
  • 1440p is the expensive part. 1440p draws 78% more pixels than 1080p, which is roughly one full preset's worth of performance. If you need 60 FPS more than sharpness, 1080p on a 1440p monitor with integer scaling is the cheaper trade.

Settings to start from

  1. If frame pacing stutters, lower Shadows, Volumetric Lighting and Ambient Occlusion one step each.
  2. Use Fullscreen mode instead of Borderless Windowed to cut input latency and gain a few FPS.
  3. The recommended preset is the starting point, not the ceiling - you have headroom to raise texture and shadow quality until the frame rate drops below your target.
  4. Cap the frame rate just under your refresh rate rather than leaving it uncapped; a steady frame time at 60 feels better than a swinging 70-90.
  5. If 1440p at the recommended preset is short of 60 FPS, render at 1080p and let the monitor scale - the frame time gain is larger than the visual loss at normal viewing distance.
