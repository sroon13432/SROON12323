Get To Work with 8 GB RAM and an RTX 2080: playable or stuttering?

Asked by the CanMySpec Spec Desk · 24 Nov 2026 · 1 answer · Answered from the official Steam requirements
Answer from the CanMySpec database — Get To Work, RTX 2080

Short answer: 8 GB meets the 8 GB minimum but falls short of the 16 GB recommended for this game. Get To Work asks for 8 GB at minimum and 16 GB at recommended settings, and with RTX 2080 the memory question is separable from the graphics one.

What limits you first

  • Memory is at the minimum, not the recommendation. With 8 GB against a 16 GB recommendation, background apps (browser tabs, Discord, recording software) are competing with the game. Close them before you judge the frame rate.
  • Storage is a size question, not a speed one. The store page lists 5 GB available space; the publisher does not require an SSD, so free space is the only thing to check.
  • The CPU assumption. Every frame rate on this page assumes a processor at or above the published minimum, Intel Core i5-8250u. If yours is older, the GPU figure is the ceiling and the CPU decides how far below it you land.
  • 1440p is the expensive part. 1440p draws 78% more pixels than 1080p, which is roughly one full preset's worth of performance. If you need 60 FPS more than sharpness, 1080p on a 1440p monitor with integer scaling is the cheaper trade.

Settings to start from

  1. If frame pacing stutters, lower Shadows, Volumetric Lighting and Ambient Occlusion one step each.
  2. Use Fullscreen mode instead of Borderless Windowed to cut input latency and gain a few FPS.
  3. Keep the recommended preset and drop shadow quality one notch: it is the single largest cost in most modern renderers and the least visible in motion.
  4. Leave ray tracing off unless you have a card that supports hardware upscaling with it; the reflections are not worth half your frame rate on this tier.
  5. If 1440p at the recommended preset is short of 60 FPS, render at 1080p and let the monitor scale - the frame time gain is larger than the visual loss at normal viewing distance.
