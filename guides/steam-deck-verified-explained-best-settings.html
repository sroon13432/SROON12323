Steam Deck Verified Explained: Best Settings for 40-60 FPS

Updated 16 November 2026 9 min read 1,238 words · data from 9,459 verified game requirement sets

Verified does not mean smooth. Here is what each Deck badge guarantees, and the exact TDP, refresh and upscaling settings that keep a heavy game at 40 FPS.

The Steam Deck badge is the single most useful compatibility signal on Steam, and it is also the most misunderstood. Verified does not mean the game holds 60 frames per second, and Unsupported does not mean it will not run. The badge describes how much of the console experience Valve could guarantee: readable text, working controls, no configuration dialogs, and no warnings.

This guide separates what each badge promises from what it does not, then gives the settings that turn a demanding Verified game into a stable 40 frames per second, which is the number most Deck owners end up preferring over 60.

The four badges, in plain terms

BadgeWhat it meansWhat it does not mean
VerifiedLaunches without manual configuration, controller and on-screen keyboard work, text is legible at 1280x800, no compatibility warnings and no missing Proton components60 FPS. A Verified badge can be attached to a game that runs at 28 FPS on low
PlayableRuns, but needs a manual step: picking a community controller layout, opening a launcher, using the on-screen keyboard, or reading small textUnplayable. Most Playable titles run well once you configure them once
UnsupportedDoes not currently run correctly through Proton, usually because of anti-cheat or a launcher dependencyImpossible. Some Unsupported games work with a different Proton build, and anti-cheat support changes over time
UnknownValve has not tested itAnything at all in either direction

In our own database, 2,210 games carry the Verified rating, 3,651 are Playable and 3,597 are Unsupported, out of 9,459 tracked. That is roughly a quarter Verified, which is a much smaller share than the store badge makes it feel, and it is why checking before you buy matters.

Why 40 FPS beats 60 on Deck

The Deck's screen refreshes at 60 Hz, and it can also run at 40 Hz. The second option exists because 40 FPS at a 40 Hz refresh divides evenly, so every frame is displayed for exactly one refresh and the result is completely even pacing with no judder. A game that averages 47 FPS at 60 Hz produces visible stutter; the same game locked to 40 FPS at 40 Hz looks smooth.

The second reason is power. Rendering 40 frames per second instead of 60 cuts the GPU workload by a third, which translates directly into lower fan speed, lower temperature and often an extra forty minutes to an hour of battery. On the OLED model the difference is smaller but still real.

The rule most owners settle on

Light games: 60 FPS capped. Anything demanding: 40 FPS at 40 Hz. Never leave a heavy game uncapped, because the Deck will spend the session throttling and frame pacing will wander.

The settings that actually do the work

  1. Press the Quick Access button (the ... button), open Performance, and turn on the Framerate Limit. Set it to 40 and set the refresh rate to 40 Hz on the same screen.
  2. Set TDP Limit to between 8 and 10 W for most demanding titles. Above that the Deck is already thermally limited, and a lower limit gives you the same frames at a lower temperature and fan speed.
  3. In the game itself, set the resolution to 1280x800 or 1280x720 and enable FSR or the game's own upscaler at quality mode. Rendering at 720p and scaling to 800p is the single biggest gain available.
  4. Turn off MSAA and high shadows, and set textures to medium. The Deck's 16 GB of shared memory is generous for its GPU class, so textures rarely need to drop below medium.
  5. In the Performance overlay, enable the frame time graph for a session. A flat line means you found the right limit, a sawtooth means the cap is too high for the scene.

For games with a built-in benchmark or a busy opening scene, test the cap there rather than in a quiet menu. A cap that holds in the tutorial and collapses in the first city is not a cap, it is a guess.

When a game is Playable, or Unsupported but still works

  • Playable usually means a launcher or a text-size problem. Open the game once in desktop mode, complete the launcher, then return to game mode and it will usually behave.
  • For controller problems, open the Steam Input menu and apply a community layout. The top-rated layouts are usually better than the default.
  • For Unsupported games, open Properties > Compatibility and force a specific Proton version, including Proton Experimental or a GE-Proton build installed through a compatibility tool manager. This fixes a large share of older titles.
  • Anti-cheat is the wall that cannot be worked around. If a competitive multiplayer game does not support Proton, no setting changes that, and bypassing it is a ban risk not worth taking.
  • ProtonDB lists player-reported results per game and per Proton version. It is the fastest way to know whether a specific title is worth the effort before you install 90 GB.

Battery, thermals and storage

The LCD Deck draws around 25 W total at full tilt, and the OLED model is more efficient at the same frame rate. In practice you will see between 90 minutes and two and a half hours in a demanding title, and four to six hours in a 2D or older game. The TDP limit is the lever that controls this: 6 to 8 W is enough for most indie games at 60 FPS, and it more than doubles runtime.

Storage matters more on Deck than on a desktop because games get installed and removed constantly. A 64 GB model fills with one AAA game and its shader cache, so an SD card is effectively mandatory. Buy an A2-rated card: the difference in texture streaming between a slow card and a good one is visible in open-world games, and DirectStorage titles will not use a card at all.

Frequently asked questions

Does Steam Deck Verified mean 60 FPS?

No. The badge covers launch behaviour, controls, text legibility and compatibility warnings, not performance. A Verified game can run at 30 FPS on low settings. Always check a gameplay video or ProtonDB report for the frame rate before buying a demanding title.

Is 40 FPS better than 60 FPS on Steam Deck?

For demanding games, usually yes. The Deck can run its screen at 40 Hz, so 40 FPS divides evenly and looks perfectly smooth, while a game fluctuating around 50 FPS at 60 Hz looks worse despite the higher number. 40 FPS also uses noticeably less power, which means a cooler machine and longer battery life.

Can I play Unsupported games on Steam Deck?

Sometimes. Unsupported usually means a launcher problem or an anti-cheat block. Forcing a different Proton version in the game's Compatibility settings fixes many older titles, but anti-cheat in competitive multiplayer games cannot be worked around safely, and attempting it risks a ban.

How much TDP should I set on Steam Deck?

Between 6 and 10 W for most games. Indie and 2D titles run at 60 FPS on 6 W, demanding 3D games usually settle between 8 and 10 W. Setting it higher mostly produces heat and fan noise rather than frames, because the chip is thermally limited anyway.
