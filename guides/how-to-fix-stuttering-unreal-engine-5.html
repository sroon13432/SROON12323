How to Fix Stuttering and Low FPS in Unreal Engine 5 Games

Updated 19 November 2026 11 min read 1,409 words · data from 18,489 verified game requirement sets

UE5 stutter has two causes and they have different fixes. Diagnose which one you have, then work through the settings, driver and engine tweaks in the right order.

Unreal Engine 5 produces two kinds of stutter and they are not the same problem. Shader compilation stutter happens the first time the game shows you a new effect: the engine compiles the shader on the CPU, the frame takes a hundred milliseconds, and you feel a hitch. Traversal stutter happens when you move through the world and the engine has to load new assets faster than the storage can deliver them. One is fixed by warming the cache; the other is fixed by lowering texture streaming pressure or moving the game to faster storage.

Working in the wrong order wastes hours, because most of the tweaks recommended online address one of these and do nothing for the other. This is the order that isolates the cause first.

Step 1 - Identify which stutter you have

SymptomLikely causeFirst fix
Hitches in the first minutes, then smoothShader compilationLet the game finish compiling; do not skip the shader step; keep the shader cache
Repeats in the same spot every sessionShader compilation with a cache that is not persistingIncrease the driver shader cache size, verify game files
Hitches when entering a new area or turning quicklyTraversal and asset streamingFaster storage, lower texture and streaming settings, more VRAM headroom
Constant low frame rate with a stable frame timeNot stutter at all, just GPU or CPU limitedLower the settings or the resolution
Hitches only after an hour of playMemory pressure or thermal throttlingCheck VRAM and RAM commit, check temperatures

Use a frame time graph rather than an average frame counter. In Steam, enable the performance overlay; for anything outside Steam, CapFrameX or the MSI Afterburner overlay with frame time logging will do. A single spike of 80 to 150 ms once per new effect is shader compilation. A cluster of spikes whenever the camera moves is streaming.

Step 2 - The fixes that cost nothing

  • Let the opening sequence play. Many UE5 games compile shaders during the first area; skipping straight to the menu leaves the work to be done mid-game.
  • Disable overlays you are not using: Discord, GeForce Experience, the Xbox Game Bar, and any RGB or monitoring utility. Overlay hooks are a common cause of periodic hitches in UE5 titles.
  • Set the Windows power plan to Balanced or High performance and confirm the GPU is not in a power-saving state. On laptops, plug in.
  • Turn on Resizable BAR / Smart Access Memory in the BIOS if the platform supports it. It matters more in UE5 than in most engines because of how much texture data the engine streams.
  • Cap the frame rate a few frames below your refresh rate and enable VRR. An uncapped frame rate makes the engine render ahead and amplifies both kinds of stutter.

Step 3 - The settings that actually cost frames

UE5's expensive features are well identified and each has a cheaper substitute. In roughly descending order of cost:

SettingCostWhat to use instead
Lumen global illumination and reflectionsVery high, 25 to 45 percentSwitch to the non-Lumen or screen-space mode if the game offers it; keep it if it is the whole look of the game
Hardware ray tracingHigh, 30 to 60 percentOff, or on with an upscaler at performance mode
Virtual shadow mapsHighMedium shadows; this is often the single largest win in UE5
Nanite geometryModerate on GPU, heavy on VRAMUsually no toggle; reduce texture quality to free memory
Texture quality and streaming poolBandwidth and VRAMOne step down from maximum; this is what fixes traversal stutter on 8 GB cards
Post-processing: motion blur, depth of field, bloomLow each, adds upDisable motion blur first, it is also the most disliked
Order matters

Change one setting, play the same 60 seconds, and look at the frame time graph. Changing five at once tells you that something helped but not what, and you will end up with a worse-looking game than necessary.

Step 4 - Upscalers and frame generation

DLSS, FSR and XeSS all reduce the render resolution and reconstruct the image, which raises the frame rate and also reduces VRAM pressure at the same time. On a card at the edge of a game's requirements, quality mode is usually free performance. Frame generation is a different tool: it raises the displayed frame rate without improving responsiveness, and it needs a base frame rate above about 45 to feel acceptable. On a game that stutters, frame generation makes the stutter look worse, because each hitch is now displayed twice.

Use upscaling to fix a low frame rate. Do not use frame generation to fix stutter; fix the stutter first and then decide whether you want the extra smoothness.

Step 5 - Driver, cache and engine-level fixes

  1. Set the NVIDIA shader cache size to 10 GB or unlimited in the NVIDIA Control Panel (Manage 3D Settings > Shader Cache Size). The default 4 GB is evicted constantly by modern games.
  2. Update the graphics driver, and then use the vendor's clean install option once if you have upgraded across several driver generations. Corrupted shader caches survive normal updates.
  3. In Steam, verify the integrity of the game files. A partially downloaded shader pack produces exactly the stutter you are trying to fix.
  4. Move the game to an NVMe SSD. If it is already on one, check that it is not over 90 percent full: a full drive slows write-back and worsens streaming.
  5. Raise the Windows page file to a fixed 16 to 32 GB on the SSD if the system has 16 GB of RAM. UE5 titles commit a great deal of virtual memory and a small page file produces periodic stalls.

Engine.ini edits that appear in every forum thread, such as streaming pool size overrides or shader compilation toggles, are worth trying only after the steps above, and only with a backup of the file. Many of them are placeholders that do nothing in a shipping build, and a few make things worse by forcing more work onto a GPU that was already the limit. Treat any specific value you find as a starting point to test, not a fix.

Step 6 - When the hardware is the honest answer

If the frame time graph is flat but the frame rate is 35, or if texture quality visibly degrades as you turn, the game is asking for hardware you do not have. The two figures to check are VRAM and system RAM: UE5 streaming stutter on an 8 GB card at 1440p is not a settings problem, it is a capacity problem, and dropping textures one step is the only real fix short of a new card.

Frequently asked questions

Why do Unreal Engine 5 games stutter on a good PC?

Usually shader compilation. UE5 compiles shaders at runtime the first time an effect appears, which produces a frame that takes far longer than the others. A game that compiles everything during a loading screen or a first-run step does not stutter; a game that compiles on the fly does, regardless of how fast the GPU is.

Does a faster SSD fix stuttering?

It fixes traversal stutter, where the game hitches while streaming assets as you move, and it does nothing for shader compilation stutter. If the hitches happen in the same place every session while you turn or walk, faster storage and a lower texture setting will help. If they happen once per new effect and then stop, storage is not the cause.

Should I use frame generation to get rid of stutter?

No. Frame generation displays interpolated frames between rendered ones, so a hitch becomes two hitches and input latency rises. Fix the frame time first with settings or upscaling, then enable frame generation only if you want a higher displayed frame rate on top of a base above about 45 FPS.

What is the single most effective UE5 setting to change?

For most systems it is the shadow setting, specifically dropping virtual shadow maps to medium. It is expensive in exactly the way UE5 is expensive, and the visual difference at 1080p to 1440p is small compared with the frames it returns. On 8 GB cards, texture quality is the close second.
