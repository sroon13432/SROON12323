Best Budget GPUs for 1080p 60 FPS in 2026

Updated 18 November 2026 10 min read 1,617 words · data from 15,488 verified game requirement sets

Three cards still define the budget tier in 2026. Here is the honest comparison: where each one wins, where 8 GB hurts, and which upgrade is worth the money.

The 1080p 60 FPS card is the most bought graphics card in PC gaming, and in 2026 the shortlist has barely moved: the RTX 3060 12GB, the RTX 4060 8GB and the RX 6600 8GB still cover the overwhelming majority of budget builds, new and used. All three are capable of 1080p 60 FPS. They differ in what happens when a game asks for more memory than they have, and in how much of that 60 FPS you get without touching a settings menu.

This comparison is built from published specifications, the launch prices each card was sold at, and the requirement data of 15,488 games, matched card by card. It deliberately avoids quoting a street price: those move weekly and depend on where you buy. Instead it tells you what each card gives you for the money, so you can judge today's price yourself.

Two things stand out. The RTX 4060 has the smallest memory bus and the lowest bandwidth of the three, and it is still the fastest card here, because its large L2 cache absorbs most of what the narrow bus would otherwise cost. And the RTX 3060 carries 50 percent more VRAM than either rival, which matters far more in 2026 than it did when the card launched.

Performance at 1080p: how big is the gap

Averaged across reviews at 1080p with no ray tracing, the RTX 4060 leads the RTX 3060 by roughly 15 to 25 percent, and the RTX 3060 leads the RX 6600 by roughly 5 to 12 percent. The spread is wide because the answer depends entirely on the engine: titles that are memory-bandwidth heavy favour the 3060, titles with heavy shaders and large caches favour the 4060, and CPU-limited esports titles show almost no difference at all.

In practice that means all three cards deliver 60 FPS at 1080p high in most releases from before 2024, and all three need help in the heaviest 2025 and 2026 titles. The help differs. The RTX 4060 can lean on DLSS Frame Generation, which is genuinely useful at this tier because it turns a 45 FPS experience into something that feels like 70. The RX 6600 cannot generate frames at all, so it has to drop settings. The RTX 3060 usually has the memory to keep textures high while it drops everything else.

The 8 GB problem, stated plainly

Both the RTX 4060 and the RX 6600 carry 8 GB. At 1080p with high textures that is still enough for most releases, but a growing number of 2025 and 2026 titles want more than 8 GB for high textures at 1440p, and a few want it at 1080p once ray tracing is enabled. When a card runs out of VRAM the frame counter does not simply drop; frame times spike, textures pop between low and high resolution, and the experience becomes uneven in a way that no settings preset fixes.

  • At 1080p high, 8 GB is workable in most games if you keep textures at high rather than ultra.
  • At 1080p with ray tracing, 8 GB is the first thing that fails in the heaviest titles.
  • At 1440p, 8 GB is the limiting factor on both cards before the GPU itself is.
  • If you keep a card for four years, the 12 GB card ages better than its frame rate suggests.
Decision rule

1080p only, and you upgrade every two to three years: 8 GB is fine. 1440p, or you keep hardware for five years: buy the card with 12 GB or more, whatever the frame rate difference today.

Power, size and the rest of the build

The RTX 4060 draws 115 W and will run on a 450 W power supply, which makes it the easiest of the three to drop into an older prebuilt. The RX 6600 needs 132 W and a 450 W supply as well. The RTX 3060 wants 170 W and a 550 W supply to be comfortable. All three use a single 8-pin connector and fit in a two-slot space, so case clearance is rarely the issue; the power supply is.

One more practical difference: the RTX 4060 has the strictest CPU requirement of the three in spirit. Its driver overhead is heavier on old four-core processors, and pairing it with a decade-old CPU can leave performance on the table. The RX 6600 has the lightest driver overhead on Windows in several titles, which makes it the more forgiving card for a very old platform.

Before you buy: a five-point check

Run through this against the specific card and price in front of you. It catches the mismatches that a specification table hides.

  1. The power supply is at least 450 W for the RTX 4060 or RX 6600, 550 W for the RTX 3060, and the unit is under eight years old.
  2. The monitor is 1080p, or you have accepted that 1440p means upscaling and a preset down from high.
  3. Your CPU is not more than about six generations old, or the card will wait on it in exactly the games you want to play.
  4. Your case fits a two-slot card around 240 mm long, and the 8-pin cable reaches.
  5. You know whether you are buying new with a warranty or used without one, because that changes what the cheapest option actually costs.

Which one to buy

New, at or near launch price, for a straight 1080p 60 FPS build: the RTX 4060. It is the fastest of the three, it uses the least power, DLSS Frame Generation genuinely helps at this tier, and it fits almost any existing power supply.

New, for a 1440p monitor or for a machine you intend to keep for years: the RTX 3060 12GB still makes sense when it is meaningfully cheaper than the 4060, because the memory is what keeps it usable. If neither is discounted, the next tier up is the better purchase.

Used, or on a very tight budget with an old CPU: the RX 6600 is the value pick of the generation. It gives most of the 3060's raster performance for less, and it is the most tolerant of an older platform. Check that it is not an ex-mining card and that the fans and thermal pads are intact.

Also worth a look

The Intel Arc B580 sits just above this tier with 12 GB of memory, and the RX 7600 sits between the 6600 and the 4060. Both are worth checking against today's price, because on a per-frame basis either can beat all three cards above depending on the week.

Frequently asked questions

Is the RTX 4060 worth it over the RTX 3060 in 2026?

For 1080p at high settings and long sessions, usually yes: it is roughly 15 to 25 percent faster at 1080p, draws 55 W less, and can use DLSS Frame Generation. The exception is a 1440p display or heavy texture work, where the 3060's 12 GB keeps it smoother than the raw frame rate suggests.

Is 8 GB of VRAM enough for 1080p gaming in 2026?

Enough for most games at high textures without ray tracing, and not enough for the heaviest 2026 titles at 1440p or with ray tracing enabled. If you keep graphics cards for four or five years, buying 12 GB or more is the safer choice regardless of today's benchmark gap.

Can the RX 6600 do 1080p 60 FPS?

Yes, in most releases from before 2024 at high settings, and in newer games at medium. It has no frame generation and weaker ray tracing than the RTX cards, so it needs a settings drop sooner, but it is the cheapest way into this performance tier.

Does a budget card need a new power supply?

Usually not for these three. The RTX 4060 needs about 450 W and one 8-pin connector, the RX 6600 the same, and the RTX 3060 about 550 W. Check the 12 V rail rating on the label rather than the marketing wattage, and replace the unit if it is more than eight years old.
